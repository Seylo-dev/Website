Each node runs Traefik on ports 80 and 443 (or shares the one Wings runs, see [Running next to Wings](/guides/running-next-to-wings)). A service's **Domains** tab decides which hostnames reach it.

## Automatic domains

Give each node a wildcard DNS record once:

```text
*.de1.example.com   A   203.0.113.10
```

and set `de1.example.com` as the node's **Wildcard domain** in its settings on **Nodes**. From then on the **Domains** tab of a service on that node offers `<service>.de1.example.com`: click **Add** and it works right away, with no DNS change. Templates add one by themselves.

Certificates come from Let's Encrypt over HTTP, one per hostname, so the panel never needs a DNS provider token.

## Custom domains

Click **Add domain**, enter the hostname and the **Container port**. Left empty, it is the service's port: its first TCP port, or for an app built from a repository with none, the port its other domains use, else 80, the same `PORT` it gets (changed in 0.2.3: before, it suggested 3000). In **Edit routing**, an empty field keeps the domain's port. Changed in 0.2.12: the field takes any port, also one the app listens on without declaring it. On a service with TCP ports, the arrow at the end of the field lists them to pick from; before, the field was a list of those ports only. The row shows the record to create where the domain's DNS is managed: an `A` record (and `AAAA` when the node has IPv6) pointing at the node. When the zone is on Cloudflare, **Open Cloudflare DNS** opens Cloudflare with the record filled in; the panel never asks for a Cloudflare API token.

The panel checks the record through public resolvers and shows one of:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| DNS ok | The domain points at the node |
| Waiting for DNS | No record yet |
| DNS points elsewhere | The record points at another address |
| Behind Cloudflare proxy | Proxied by Cloudflare, so DNS shows Cloudflare's addresses. The origin check below tells whether Cloudflare reaches the node. |

New in 0.2.5: each row names the IP its record should point at, next to the status.

### No route on the node

New in 0.2.13. A node routes a service's domains once a deployment of the service is live there. When the panel knows a domain has no route on its node, the row says why in place of **Certificate waits for DNS** or **Certificate waits for Cloudflare**, which read as a record to fix while the record was right:

| The row reads | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Not deployed yet** | The service has not deployed yet: its first build is queued, building or failed, or its first rollout failed, so nothing of it runs on the node, routes included. The note says its domains go live, and their certificates are asked for, once a deployment goes live, with a **Deployments** button. |
| **Waiting for the first deploy** | The same, while its first deployment is on its way. |
| **No route on `<node>`** | The node could not write the service's routes, for example because a host is routed by another service on the node. The note gives the node's own error ("`<node>` could not write this service's routes: ...") and says the node keeps serving the routes it wrote before and tries again every 10 seconds. Before, the error only showed in the agent's journal. |
| **No route** | The service is a compose stack or a cron service, which take no domains (below). |

While a domain has no route, the row offers no **Regenerate certificate**. While the service has not deployed, or is a stack or a cron service, the panel checks nothing through Cloudflare for its domains, since no check could pass; once a deployment goes live, or the node writes the routes it could not, it checks the waiting domains at once. Adding a domain to a service that has not deployed says it goes live once the service deploys.

The node's error comes from nodes with agent 0.2.13. On the node, `seylo status` lists the service under **Recent errors** as `routes of <service>`, with "(still failing)", until a write works.

Compose stacks and cron services take no domains: a stack runs no container of its own, so a domain belongs on the stack member that serves it, and a cron service serves no requests. Neither has a **Domains** tab, and `domains.add`, `domains.addAuto` and `domains.update` refuse them (reason `stackParent` or `cronService`). A stack or a cron service with domains from before shows a **Domains** tab with only those, each saying to put it on the stack member that serves it, or to remove it.

In the API and MCP, `domains.list` and `domains.add` return `noRoute` with its `reason` (`notDeployed`, `stackParent`, `cronService` or `routeWriteFailed`, the last with the node and its error), and `domains.retryCertificate` refuses while the service has not deployed, and on a stack or a cron service.

### Behind Cloudflare's proxy

New in 0.2.5: for a proxied domain the panel also runs an **origin check**. It asks `http://<domain>/.well-known/seylo-origin/<random>` and the node's agent answers with a signature only that node can make, so the panel knows whether the record in Cloudflare reaches this service's node, not just Cloudflare. The check runs a few seconds after you add the domain, on **Check again** or **Check now** on the row, and before **Regenerate certificate**. The row's chip, **Cloudflare proxy → `<IP>`**, names the node's IP, the address the record in Cloudflare must point at.

- The check reached the node: the chip turns green with a check. The record in Cloudflare is right; the certificate follows.
- Something else answered (an old server, another host): the chip turns red and reads **Cloudflare proxy, not this node**. Fix the record in Cloudflare to point at the IP the row shows. Until then **Regenerate certificate** refuses, since Let's Encrypt reaches the domain the same way and would fail.
- No answer that tells (a timeout, Cloudflare's 52x, an agent older than 0.2.5): the panel cannot tell where the record points. New in 0.2.13: when the answer came from Cloudflare itself, the row says what stopped the check (see [Certificate waits for Cloudflare](#certificate-waits-for-cloudflare)).

Changed in 0.2.13: a challenge or a block from Cloudflare's own security never counts as another server answering, since the request may not have left Cloudflare. Before, its 403 page read **Cloudflare proxy, not this node** while the record was right. A domain with **HTTPS** set to **HTTP only** now answers the check on port 443 as well, so behind **Always Use HTTPS** with SSL/TLS on **Full** it no longer reads **Cloudflare proxy, not this node** either.

New in 0.2.8: a custom domain does not ask Let's Encrypt for its certificate until it reaches the node: its record points at the node, or behind Cloudflare the origin check reached the node. Until then the row says **Certificate waits for DNS** (behind Cloudflare's proxy, since 0.2.13, **Certificate waits for Cloudflare**), the route serves the node's own certificate, and no failed validation counts against Let's Encrypt's limit of 5 per host name per hour. The panel checks waiting domains again on its own, and **Check again** on the row checks at once; the certificate starts as soon as one says the domain arrived. Automatic domains (the node's wildcard record) and domains with their own certificate never wait. Also fixed in 0.2.8: a proxied domain whose certificate was issued kept showing **Generating**.

Cloudflare's free certificate covers your domain and one level of subdomain (`example.com`, `*.example.com`), not deeper ones such as `app.eu1.example.com`. For a node's automatic domains under `*.eu1.example.com`, set that wildcard record to DNS only (grey cloud) so visitors get the node's Let's Encrypt certificate. The check only connects to public addresses or this panel's own nodes, never to a private network a domain may resolve to.

As soon as the domain reaches the node, Traefik requests a certificate and the route goes live. A certificate that cannot be issued shows **Certificate failed** with the reason from Let's Encrypt, and raises an alert. One that stays on **Generating** behind the proxy says why under the row, with **Check again**: the record does not reach the node yet, what Cloudflare answered the panel's check (below), or, 10 minutes after the domain was added, Cloudflare's settings. That last note still says to turn off **Always Use HTTPS**, though it does not stop Let's Encrypt's check (see the callout below): look first at a Redirect Rule or Page Rule for every path, on a node with an agent older than 0.2.13, and at [Cloudflare's security](#certificate-waits-for-cloudflare).

<Callout type="warn" title="Cloudflare proxy">
With Cloudflare's proxy (orange cloud) on, set SSL/TLS to **Full (strict)**. Flexible mode sends plain HTTP to the node, which redirects to HTTPS, which loops. **Always Use HTTPS** can stay on: it leaves Let's Encrypt's check of `http://<domain>/.well-known/acme-challenge/` on plain HTTP, so it does not stop certificates or renewals. The one time it gets in the way is a new domain's first certificate on **Full (strict)**: turn it off, or use **Full**, until the certificate is issued (see [Certificate waits for Cloudflare](#certificate-waits-for-cloudflare)). Since 0.2.13, on nodes with agent 0.2.13, a Redirect Rule or Page Rule that sends every path to HTTPS does not stop certificates either, apart from that same first certificate on **Full (strict)** (see [Redirects to HTTPS in Cloudflare](#redirects-to-https-in-cloudflare)). **Redirect HTTP to HTTPS** on the domain sends visitors to HTTPS either way.
</Callout>

### Certificate waits for Cloudflare

New in 0.2.13. While a custom domain behind Cloudflare's proxy waits for the origin check, its row reads **Certificate waits for Cloudflare**, unless the domain has no route on the node (see [No route on the node](#no-route-on-the-node)). Before, it read **Certificate waits for DNS**, with the same note whatever stopped the check. The record in Cloudflare exists; what holds the certificate up is that the panel's check through Cloudflare has not reached the node yet. Until it does, the domain is served over plain HTTP.

Under the row a note says what to fix, next to **Check again**, which runs the DNS and origin check at once. The panel also checks the domain again on its own, and once a minute while the **Domains** tab is open. When Cloudflare itself answered the check (its `cf-ray` or `server: cloudflare` header, or the `cf-mitigated` header of a challenge page), the note says why the check did not reach the node:

| Cloudflare answered | What it means | What the note says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 525 or 526 | SSL/TLS **Full (strict)** refuses the node's temporary certificate | "Cloudflare's SSL mode Full (strict) refuses the node's temporary certificate until Let's Encrypt issues one: set SSL/TLS to Full for a few minutes, or turn off Always Use HTTPS, then Check again." |
| 521 or 522 | Cloudflare cannot connect to the node | "Cloudflare cannot reach the node on port 443/80. Its record for `<domain>` must point at `<IP>`, and a firewall in front of the node must let Cloudflare in on those ports. Then Check again." |
| 404 | The server Cloudflare reaches has nothing for the check | "Requests to `<domain>` through Cloudflare get 404 from the server they reach, not the node's answer: its record in Cloudflare must point at `<IP>`. If it already does, the node may not have the domain yet: Check again in a minute." |
| A challenge page, or 403 or 429 | Cloudflare's security answered in the node's place | "Cloudflare's security answered the check with a challenge or a block instead of passing it to the node (Bot Fight Mode, Under Attack mode, or a WAF or rate limiting rule), and it can stop Let's Encrypt the same way. Let requests to /.well-known/ on `<domain>` through (a WAF skip rule, or Bot Fight Mode off), then Check again." |

Any other answer gives no reason, and the note is the general one: point the record in Cloudflare at the node's IP, and if it already does and SSL/TLS is on **Full (strict)**, turn off **Always Use HTTPS** until the certificate is issued. A 404 also shows under **Cloudflare proxy, not this node**, below the record to fix.

**Full (strict) and the first certificate.** While the domain waits, the node serves HTTPS with Traefik's own certificate, since it has asked Let's Encrypt for nothing yet. The panel's check goes to `http://`; Always Use HTTPS sends it on to `https://`, Cloudflare connects to the node on port 443, and Full (strict) refuses that certificate. On **Full** Cloudflare accepts it, and with Always Use HTTPS off the check reaches the node on port 80. Either way, click **Check again**. Once the row shows **Certificate valid until** a date, set back what you changed: renewals pass with **Full (strict)** and **Always Use HTTPS** on. A Redirect Rule or Page Rule that sends every path to `https://` sends the check to port 443 just like Always Use HTTPS, so with one of those, turning off Always Use HTTPS is not enough: use **Full** for those minutes.

**Cloudflare's security.** Bot Fight Mode, Under Attack mode and WAF or rate limiting rules decide per request, so the check may pass once and fail the next time, and they can stop Let's Encrypt's own request to `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` the same way. Let requests whose path starts with `/.well-known/` through on the domain: a WAF custom rule with the **Skip** action for that path, or turn Bot Fight Mode off, which such a rule does not skip. That one path covers both the panel's check and Let's Encrypt's. The domain keeps waiting meanwhile and is checked again on its own.

The panel keeps the reason from its last check in memory, so right after the panel restarts (an update, for example) the note is the general one until the next check, which opening the **Domains** tab runs. In the API and MCP, `domains.list` and `domains.checkDns` return `originReason` (`cloudflareCertificate`, `cloudflareUnreachable`, `cloudflareChallenge` or `notFound`) next to the domain's `origin`.

### Redirects to HTTPS in Cloudflare

Let's Encrypt checks a domain at `http://<domain>/.well-known/acme-challenge/<token>`, for the first certificate and every renewal, and follows a redirect to `https://` without checking the certificate there.

- **Always Use HTTPS** leaves that path on plain HTTP, so Let's Encrypt reaches the node on port 80. It does not need to be off for Let's Encrypt; the only reason to turn it off for a while is the panel's check before a new domain's first certificate on **Full (strict)**, above.
- A **Redirect Rule** or **Page Rule** that sends every `http://` request to `https://` takes that path too. With SSL/TLS on **Full**, Cloudflare then sends Let's Encrypt to the node on port 443. Before 0.2.13 the app answered there (or Traefik's 404), so neither the first certificate nor any renewal came, and each failure counted against Let's Encrypt's limit of 5 failed validations per host name an hour.

New in 0.2.13: Seylo answers Let's Encrypt's challenge on port 443 too, for a domain whose DNS the panel did not see point straight at the node: behind Cloudflare's proxy, with no record or one pointing elsewhere, or not checked yet. Automatic domains, the panel's own domain and the hosts of database web UIs and Boxes always get it. A redirect for every path no longer blocks those certificates. The node needs agent 0.2.13. On **Full (strict)**, a new domain's first certificate still needs **Full** for a few minutes, as above.

On those domains, Seylo answers `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` over HTTPS itself: only the tokens of its own orders in progress, and 404 for the rest, without passing the request on. An app that answers ACME challenges itself over HTTPS on such a domain, for example a central responder that other servers redirect their challenges to, loses that path there. A domain whose record points straight at the node (DNS only, grey cloud) keeps the path for the app over HTTPS, as before, and so does a domain with a custom certificate. The panel moves a domain from one case to the other when a DNS check sees its record change.

### The panel behind Cloudflare

The panel's own domain can be proxied by Cloudflare too, with the same settings (see [Behind Cloudflare's proxy](/self-hosting/the-panel#behind-cloudflares-proxy) for how the panel checks it). Requests then reach the node from Cloudflare's addresses, not the visitor's, and Cloudflare passes the visitor's address in its `CF-Connecting-IP` header. New in 0.2: the panel reads that header. Anyone can send it, so the panel and its API believe it only on requests that came from Cloudflare's published address ranges through the node's own Traefik, its trusted proxy. On any other request, for example one sent straight to the node's IP, the header is ignored and the connection's own address counts.

That way sign-in limits, **Sessions**, the activity log and the command line approval page see each visitor's own address, instead of a few Cloudflare addresses that many people share.

### Domain options

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Path** | Only requests under this path reach the service. `/` is the whole domain. |
| **Strip the path before forwarding** | `example.com/api/users` reaches the service as `/users` |
| **HTTPS** | Let's Encrypt (default), a custom certificate, or plain HTTP only |
| **Redirect HTTP to HTTPS** | Send plain HTTP visitors to HTTPS |
| **Redirect only** | Send every request to another URL instead of the service, for example `www` to the bare domain. Choose **Permanent** and **Keep the path and query**. |
| **Make it the primary domain** | The primary domain is what `${{service.URL}}` and IP:port redirects point to. The first domain of a service is always primary, so for it the box is ticked and cannot be cleared. |

A service can have any number of domains, and each can route a path: `example.com/api` to the API and `example.com` to the web app.

### Who may use a hostname

A hostname belongs to the service that routes it. Adding it, or a path under it, to another service needs the right to edit the service that already has it, so a member can't take traffic (and its cookies) from someone else's service with a longer path. Nobody can put a service on the panel's own domain or on the hosts the nodes use for database web UIs and Boxes (`seylo-db-...` and `seylo-box-...`), and a custom certificate may not name hosts that belong elsewhere. The agent on each node enforces the same rules again.

### Custom certificates

**Upload certificate** in a domain's menu takes a PEM certificate chain and its private key; the domain switches to it. Use it for a certificate from your own CA, or a wildcard certificate you already have. It may only name hosts of this service or hosts no other service uses.

## Access rules

**Access rules** in a domain's menu add, per domain:

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Basic auth | Browsers ask for a user name and password before anything loads. Passwords need at least 8 characters and are stored hashed. |
| IP allowlist | One IP or CIDR per line. Everyone else gets 403. Empty lets everyone in. |
| Rate limit | Requests per source IP: an average, a burst, per number of seconds. Over the limit, visitors get 429. |
| Redirects | A regular expression on the full URL and its replacement; `$1` inserts a group |

## IP and port access

A new service with a port answers on the node's IP and a port the agent picks, until a domain works. The agent relays that port itself instead of publishing it through Docker, because Docker's published ports bypass ufw and similar firewalls. The **IP and port access** section on the Domains tab shows the address.

With **Close IP:port once a domain is live** on (the default), the address switches to a redirect to the primary domain once that domain is live, and closes 24 hours later. **Redirect visitors to the primary domain, keeping their session** carries cookies over through a short handoff link, so visitors stay signed in; cookie expiry and flags are set fresh by the app. **Open again** and **Close now** change it by hand.

IP:port access and other public ports relay TCP. UDP ports are coming later.

## Maintenance and down pages

Visitors see a page from the node's agent while a service is stopped, deploying for the first time or unreachable, and while maintenance mode is on.

- Per service, in **Maintenance and down pages** on the Domains tab: turn on **Maintenance mode**, set a **Message**, or **Upload custom HTML**. Without its own page, the service uses the global one.
- For everything, in **Settings → Maintenance pages**: **Maintenance mode for every service** makes every service answer with the maintenance page (HTTP 503) while the panel stays reachable. The global page is the built-in page with your message, or your own HTML.

Custom pages are full HTML documents up to 512 KB, served with a strict Content-Security-Policy: scripts and frames do not run on them, styles load inline or over HTTPS, and images and fonts over HTTPS or as `data:` URLs. New in 0.2: nodes send that policy, and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, with every page they serve, maintenance and down pages alike. On 0.1, the policy was missing, so scripts on a custom page ran on the service's own domains.

## Certificate limits

Let's Encrypt issues at most 50 certificates per registered domain per week. Many services and pull request previews under one domain can reach that. Spread them over more than one domain, or upload a wildcard certificate you already have to the domains that need it.
